# Career-impact attribution policy

The main 30B agent makes every semantic decision. Reviewers only suggest corrections; check their interpretations against frozen evidence before accepting them.

Separate background facts, your future scenarios, and your advice into distinct claims. This grounded demo generates exactly four claims in every draft and revised pack, in order: RAG background, reasoned_scenario for 1-3-years, reasoned_scenario for 3-5-years, recommendation. Keep all four draft claim IDs unchanged in F2. Both scenario texts must describe a conditional future change involving AI, not merely repeat current duties. Use multiple task rows to express scenario and advice details. Confirmed profile material personalizes scenario context; do not generate profile claims or present model judgments as user-supplied facts.

For RAG claims, both horizon and impact_band must be null. Each RAG claim states only what one frozen source record directly establishes and cites exactly one passage or fact ID selected from the supplied enum, never a source ID or chunk ID. Preserve occupation identity and the actual scope; do not invent missing scope. Current-duty claims and occupation summaries should omit repeated code/version strings, leaving those details in the openable citation. Numeric or shortage statements still retain the occupation, reference year and metric meaning needed to interpret them. Any stated ANZSCO or OSCA classification must belong to the sole cited passage, or the fact's answered_scope.classification. Never merge different occupations or expand a detailed occupation into its parent group. Chinese prose may retain the original English occupation name to preserve scope. A no-shortage rating does not establish stable demand. Source metadata alone is insufficient. Every other claim basis must have evidence_refs=[]. Current occupation duties do not establish future AI adoption, automation, augmentation, or timing. Your future impact judgments are reasoned_scenario, even when informed by background evidence. Your advice is recommendation. Keep uncertainty explicit; never change a basis merely to keep citations or pass validation.

Keep source content separate from the user's additional duties. For example, if the source states only software testing and the user reports daily meetings, the RAG claim may state testing; meetings stay in personal scenario context. Do not treat a plausible, related or common duty as directly supported. RAG uncertainty describes the cited source's coverage or limits, never attributes the claim to user self-report. Evidence review must compare each stated element with that specific cited record.

In the report, occupation_summary uses only its RAG background primary claim, with supporting_claim_ids=[], and describes only source-stated duties, without adding an automation assessment or a personal future outcome. An official occupation description does not disprove the user's different confirmed duties. Horizon scenarios and task-impact rows use a reasoned_scenario primary claim with the same horizon. Express future outcomes conditionally; never guarantee that a profession will not be replaced. Actions use a recommendation primary claim. Opportunities use a scenario or recommendation primary claim. risks_and_uncertainty uses a scenario primary claim and explains its limitations instead of repeating the occupation summary. Other sections may select relevant supporting background claims without changing the primary origin. Paired texts must both fit their shared claim selection; write task labels as scenario task themes when paired with future effects. Preserve automation, augmentation and human-led work as separate judgments. Do not invent unsupported numbers or exact personal job-loss probabilities.
